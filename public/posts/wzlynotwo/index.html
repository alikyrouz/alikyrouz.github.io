Allie X/Mariah Manser for WZLY

Ali Kyrouz
31 Oct 2024
music

Allie X concerts were meant for Halloween, and I don’t think this statement would be disputed among any of her fans. Her music lends itself to lavish costumes, and a general camp-y disconnect from the world her listeners may otherwise be living in. Although the concert that I attended occurred before our current particular brand of seasonal depression, I find it hard to believe this tour won’t continue to be a cathartic and affirming space.

So, when I learned that the second leg of the tour promoting her most recent album, Girl With No Face, would be starting in Boston on October 31st, I knew I had to go. The tour, named the “Weird World” tour after another track on the album, features Violet Chachki opening for most of its stops, but Boston was lucky enough to be the one stop to see Maiah Manser instead. Upon taking the stage at the Paradise Rock Club, Manser, a Los Angeles-based noir-pop artist, shared that it was her first time performing in Boston (despite her witch-y atmosphere being perfectly suited to Massachusetts during this time of year). Personally, I am glad that she made it for this show, and I felt that her music was a very good fit with Allie X’s discography.

Manser opened the show with her most recent single “You”, an ethereal synth-y ballad that complemented her set of orange paper flames well. Along with her staging, flames crept up Manser’s clothes as well. Her dress with LED embers on the hem could have been mistaken for a 17th century burning witch, although she took a moment to clarify that she was, in reality, a portrait of a lady on fire. Manser continued her set with tracks like “Bad Habits” and “With a Smile”, the latter of which received publicity after gaining traction on Tiktok in the past year. Vocally reminiscent of Caroline Polachek, with a persona somewhere between Ethel Cain and Florence Welch, Maiah Manser brought a perfectly quintessential Halloween soundtrack for the beginning of the show, and I look forward to hearing more of her cinematic pop tracks.

The opening of Allie X’s set introduced a taste of the eccentricity of her aesthetic to come. In a costume akin to a billowy masked magician, another figure warmed up the crowd again in a choreographed act set to Billy Idol’s “Eyes Without a Face”. With sweeping gestures, they introduced the lighting elements on stage and interacted with the audience, before leading Allie, her bassist, and her guitarist on stage.

Beginning with the track “Bitch”, from her debut 2015 album Collxtion I, Allie’s performance accelerated gradually, first facing away from the crowd in a mask of her own, before taking center stage in her signature cat-eye sunglasses, as well as a somewhat clownish buttoned and sequined corset, and poofy skirt. In reference to her outfit and apparent Halloween costume, Allie would later announce that she was “the zeitgeist”, a statement that’s pretty hard to disagree with.

Alternating between addressing the audience at the front of the stage and standing at a keyboard closer to the back, Allie played a setlist with many tracks from her most recent album, including “Weird World”, “Black Eye”, the album’s titular track “Girl With No Face”, and “Galina”, as well as older favorites like “Fresh Laundry”, “Susie Save Your Love”, and “Old Habits Die Hard”. From any location on stage, it was hard not to feel like she could see everyone individually through her frequently-present sunglasses, a notion that was aided by the clear connection and care she feels for her audience. In many interviews, Allie speaks on her awareness of younger women and queer people in her fanbase, and it was clear in her performance and interactions with the crowd that this is a sentiment she maintains.

As someone who was in middle school in 2015, my deepest association with Allie X – for better or worse – will always begin with “YOUTH”, from Troye Sivan’s debut album Blue Neighborhood, although I’ve become familiar with the rest of Allie’s work since then. For those who were not on Youtube as much as a twelve year old, the story behind the track is very much of its time: while co-writing a portion of the tracks on Blue Neighborhood, Allie, ten years Sivan’s senior and with an already-established cult fanbase, checked the inbox of her Tumblr blog to a message from a fan. The message, simply, “Allie X my youth is yours”, would inspire the chorus of “YOUTH”, the album’s lead single and Sivan’s first track to enter the top 40 of the Billboard Hot 100 chart.

To be honest, I never felt like I fully grasped the sentiment behind the quote. It seems to me that a person’s youth is both a long time and a lot of things, and I was not sure of the purpose of this phrasing other than to be a little grandiose. And although grandiosity is without a doubt a pillar of the aesthetic of Allie X’s music, I’m sure there is a more meaningful implication. Seeing her live, I realized how ready I was to completely buy into the performance and the atmosphere. A track like “Super Duper Party People”, which, despite its undeniable groove and bassline, frequently comes off a little overblown to me, sounded more like a statement of intent sung live. “Off With Her Tits”, a song that can feel rather odd to listen to on a casual walk around campus, was in its perfect setting among alternative Halloween partygoers and sequined clown leotards. It might be too late for my youth to fully belong to Allie X, but I could understand why you might try.

However, what I also found striking about her Boston show was that it did not seem like my own experience was the same as every other attendee’s relationship with her music. In fact, a large portion of the crowd were not the college students and 20-somethings of many of the shows in the Boston area, and instead seemed to come from a variety of different backgrounds. There were both men and women in their forties to their sixties, millennials one could imagine were on Tumblr in 2014, and at least one parent-child duo, as well as quite a few glittery nuns. Maybe some of them actually were the titular party people ten years ago, rather than an almost-thirteen year old on Instagram in coastal Massachusetts, but it seemed like we were all ready to embrace a little melodrama and theatrics for the ambiance.

And honestly? That’s the power of avant-garde goth art pop.
